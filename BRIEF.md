# The brief's four questions, answered with the file that proves each

The competition's post asks four questions of every entry. Each is answered here with the path a reader opens to check the answer, and with what the answer does not cover. Written on 11 September 2026, the day of the deadline, against the repository as it stands. **Dates here and throughout the repository are day/month/year**, the Brazilian order: `11/09/2026` is the eleventh of September.

**1. Does it audit against a real, citable standard, or is it just an opinion?**
Against the standard. Every report row names a provision and quotes it word for word: Lei Complementar 116/2003 (ISS: incidence, place, base, service list), LC 123/2006 (Simples Nacional), Decreto-Lei 406/1968 (fixed-amount ISS), LC 214/2025 and the Receita Federal's 2026 guidance (IBS/CBS), and the national NFS-e layout (ANEXO I, the XSD, ANEXO B). The rule that forbids opinion is in `rules.md` § 3 (a verbatim excerpt per row, never a paraphrase) and § 4 (the refusal: a request for advice is handed back); the recorded refusal is in `rounds/refusal/transcript.md`. Where the standard needs a fact the auditor does not have, the row says CANNOT DETERMINE and names what is missing (`rules.md` § 0, § 3; the run with no profile line is example 4 of `rounds/fixture-runs-v1/report-all-runs.md` — evidence for the reader, not part of what you load). What this does not cover: the office's working tables (`reference/tables/working/`) are lookups, not the standard, and no row cites them as one; the checks are the six of `rules.md` § 2 (eight rows: checks 1 and 5 each produce two), not the whole of the ISS law: the bare-model control (`rounds/control-bare-model/what-this-shows.md`) shows which parts of the law the model alone already covers, and which these checks do not.

**2. Are findings specific and located, with severity?**
Yes: one row per check per note, with the XML path, its value, and the other side of the comparison, all in the location cell; the result one of four fixed values; and, on FAIL, a severity class and who has to act (`rules.md` § 3, the closed vocabularies). The proof is the reports: `examples.md` (four worked runs on the fixtures, plus the refusal, which the file says is not an example), `rounds/round-1-v1/transcript.md` and `rounds/round-2-v2/` (the Employee running a client's note; the FAIL on the service code is named and located), `rounds/fixture-runs-v2/report.md` (the revised shape as it came). `tools/check_audit.py` also fails a row whose result or severity is not one of the allowed words, and whose location names an XML path that is not in the fixture (check C2, documented at the top of `tools/check_audit.py`). What this does not cover: severity has two classes by consequence for the month-end close; there is no numeric score, by design (`rules.md` § 3).

**3. Is the standard actually in `reference/`, where a reader can check it?**
Yes, twice over. The full texts are in `reference/pt/full/` (Portuguese, the original, with `SHA256SUMS.txt` and the capture date in `reference/INDEX.md`) and `reference/en/full/`; the excerpts the auditor loads are in `reference/pt/excerpts/` and `reference/en/excerpts/`, one file per provision. Mechanically: `python3 tools/check_audit.py` fails if any excerpt is not a verbatim substring of a full text (C0) or if any quote in any report is not a verbatim substring of `reference/` (C1, with two exemptions the code declares: the bare-model controls and the lines inside a `kept-as-came` fence are reported without gating, because both exist to preserve answers that broke a rule); `--selftest` proves it fails on a planted edit; `tools/sabotage_check.py` re-runs every act of sabotage that `tools/README.md` describes and writes down what it found in `tools/SABOTAGE-PROOF.md`. What this does not cover: the municipal rate table is an extract for one municipality (`reference/tables/municipal-rates_2800308-aracaju-se_extract-20260903.csv`, source named in `reference/tables/TABLES.md`), and the schema's field list is generated from the XSD by a script (`reference/tables/required-fields.md`, and the generator is not published in this repository), both declared as such.

**4. README quality — can a stranger figure this out?**
Measured, not claimed. The README was put in front of the Employee of a real accounting office twice, both times recorded, and rewritten to fix what she tripped on: round 1 (9 September, `rounds/round-1-v1/`) on the first version, round 2 (11 September, `rounds/round-2-v2/`) on the seventeen numbered commands the rewrite replaced it with. In round 2 she reached the first report in about ten minutes, composing the client line from her own spreadsheet. The author was on the call and helped her three times, each one declared in the transcript: to read step 3 again, to leave the spreadsheet unattached at step 15, and to replace the example's values with her client's at step 16. Her own words on the two versions are in `rounds/round-2-v2/what-changed.md`. The measure of the rewrite (words before and after, the method, the earlier versions' commits) is in `rounds/round-1-v1/what-changed.md` row 4. **And once by someone from outside the trade, alone.** On the afternoon of 11 September, hours after round 2, a person who has never worked in accounting and had never seen this repository opened the README on GitHub and followed it **with no call, nobody beside him, and no question answered**: `rounds/walk-01-stranger/`. He reached the report in **10 minutes 54 seconds** of the fifteen he was given, on the route the README teaches, and the report matched, row for row, a prediction committed to git **before he opened anything** — which `tools/prove_order.py` checks on a fresh clone, not on anyone's word. The prediction named five places where he might stop. He stopped at none, and said so himself at the end of the recording: *"Eu acabei não falando nada porque eu não tive nenhuma dúvida, não travei em nenhum lugar."*

What this does not cover: the operator is one person at one office, and the author was on the call both of her rounds. The stranger is the author's son, learning the author's method, on the author's account, handed the note and the profile line — he is declared by relation for exactly that reason, and what his walk does not prove is written in `rounds/walk-01-stranger/expected.md`, before the run, not after it.

## What would have moved this entry up a tier
One thing, and it is the same thing round 2 found: **the second note ran in the same conversation as the first.** The README says one note per conversation; she read it aloud; ten minutes later, asked for one more note, she attached it to the conversation that was already open, the natural move in a chat, and the second audit ran with the first report and the client's line still in front of it, so it was never an independent reading of the second note. Nothing in the folder could catch that, and the instruction did not say why. What would have moved this up is the auditor refusing, or at least flagging, a second XML in a conversation that already holds a report: a rule in `rules.md` § 0, with a fixture and a prediction behind it, the way the refusal has. **That one is not built, and the reason is the difference between the two kinds of rule round 2 produced.** A rule that makes the auditor refuse changes behaviour no run has ever tested: written in the last hours before a deadline, with no fixture and no prediction, it would be a claim, and the transcript that shows the gap is worth more than a claim. The other kind was built the same day: step 15 of the README now carries the reason it lacked, and `rules.md` § 3 now forbids the taker's company name in the report. That one only stops a name from being written, it takes nothing away from any check, and it has two runs of the same client in the same conversation as its evidence, one that wrote the name and one that did not. Both are rows in `rounds/round-2-v2/what-changed.md`, dated, with their commits.
