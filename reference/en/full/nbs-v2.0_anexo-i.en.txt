             FORMATION OF THE CODE OF THE BRAZILIAN NOMENCLATURE OF SERVICES,
      INTANGIBLES AND OTHER OPERATIONS THAT PRODUCE VARIATIONS IN NET WORTH (NBS)


The code in the Brazilian Nomenclature of Services, Intangibles and Other Operations that Produce Variations
in Net Worth (NBS) is composed of nine digits, whose meaning, from left to right, is:
a) The first digit is the numeral 1 and indicates that the code refers to a service, intangible or other
operation that produces a variation in net worth;
b) the second and third digits, together with the first, indicate a chapter of the NBS;
c) the fourth and fifth digits, together with the digits representing a chapter, indicate a heading
within that chapter;
d) the sixth and seventh digits, together with the digits representing a heading, indicate,
respectively, the first- and second-level subheadings of that heading;
e) the eighth digit is the item; and
f) the ninth digit is the sub-item.
(Fl. 5 of Annex I of Joint Ordinance RFB/SCS No.     , of December, 2018.)


 GENERAL RULES FOR THE INTERPRETATION OF THE BRAZILIAN NOMENCLATURE OF SERVICES, INTANGIBLES AND
            OTHER OPERATIONS THAT PRODUCE VARIATIONS IN NET WORTH (RGS)


The classification of services in the Brazilian Nomenclature of Services, Intangibles and Other Operations that
Produce Variations in Net Worth (NBS) is governed by the following Rules:


Rule 1. The titles of Sections and Chapters are provided for ease of reference only. For legal purposes,
classification is determined by the texts of the headings and of the Section and Chapter Notes and, provided
they are not contrary to the texts of said headings and Notes, by the following Rules.


Rule 2. When it appears that the service, intangible or other operation that produces variations in net worth
could be classified under two or more headings, classification shall be carried out as follows:
 a) The heading providing the most specific description is to be preferred to headings providing a more
 general description. However, when two or more headings each refer to only one of the services, intangibles
 and other operations that produce variations in net worth that constitute the object to be classified, such
 headings are to be regarded as equally specific in relation to that object, even if one of them gives a more
 complete or precise description of it than the others.
 b) When Rule 2a) does not permit classification to be made, the service, intangible or other operation that
 produces variations in net worth shall be classified under the heading which occurs last in numerical order,
 among those which equally merit consideration.

Rule 3. The classification of a service, intangible or other operation that produces variations in net worth in the
subheadings of a single heading is determined, for legal purposes, by the texts of those subheadings and of
the relative Subheading Notes and, mutatis mutandis, by the preceding Rules, on the understanding that only
subheadings at the same level are comparable. For the purposes of this Rule, the Section and Chapter Notes
also apply, unless otherwise provided.

Rule 4. The foregoing rules shall apply, mutatis mutandis, to determine, within each subheading, the
applicable item and, within the latter, the corresponding sub-item, on the understanding that only
breakdowns (items and sub-items) at the same level are comparable.
(Fl. 6 of Annex I of Joint Ordinance RFB/SCS No.      , of December, 2018.)

    SECTION II - INTERMEDIATION SERVICES IN THE DISTRIBUTION OF GOODS;
CUSTOMS CLEARANCE SERVICES; PROVISION OF FOOD AND BEVERAGES AND ACCOMMODATION SERVICES;
        TRANSPORT SERVICES AND PUBLIC UTILITY DISTRIBUTION SERVICES

Notes

1) In this section:
  a) Inland navigation is waterborne transport carried out in areas sheltered or partially sheltered
  from bad weather, where storms occurring on the open sea do not affect or barely affect it,
  such as bays, coves, lagoons, rivers, lakes, canals etc., on domestic or international routes.
  b) Coastal navigation is waterborne transport carried out in open-sea regions where it is still possible
  to sight the coast, limited to a maximum of 20 (twenty) nautical miles from the coast and, in principle, does not
  require the use of orientation instruments, since navigation is carried out by observing land.
(Fl. 10 of Annex I of Joint Ordinance RFB/SCS No.    , of December, 2018.)


 c) Transoceanic navigation is waterborne transport carried out between domestic and foreign
 ports, beyond the limits of visibility of the coast and without other established limits. It requires the use of
 analog or digital location and orientation instruments.
 d) Multimodal freight transport is that which uses two or more modes of transport, from
 origin to destination, and is governed by a single contract, being carried out under the responsibility of
 a single multimodal transport operator.
 e) Multimodal transport operator is a legal entity engaged as the principal party responsible for
 carrying out the multimodal transport of goods from origin to destination, by its own means (the
 operator's own) or through third parties, and may or may not be the carrier of the goods.




     Chapter 2 - Intermediation services in the distribution of goods; customs clearance
                                         services; trade


Notes

1) "Intermediation services in the distribution of goods" cover, exclusively, the
activities of intermediating third-party goods in wholesale and retail trade and the services
provided by commissioned agents. Thus, the purchases and sales of the goods that are the object of such
intermediation are not included in the Brazilian Nomenclature of Services (NBS).
2) Trade, both wholesale and retail, is taken, exclusively, within the scope of this
Nomenclature as a mixed operation because it involves a set of services with delivery of
goods, ownership of which belongs to the party carrying out those commercial activities.
3) "Customs clearance services" are those related to the clearance of goods or
merchandise, including travelers' baggage, on import or export, transported by any
means.
4) In the context of heading 1.0205, with respect to operations carried out under the jurisdiction and in accordance with the
Brazilian regulations, the trading of electricity as a good, classified as a good in the
Mercosur Common Nomenclature (NCM), may only be carried out within the scope of the Electric Energy
Trading Chamber (CCEE).

              Chapter 3 - Provision of food and beverages and accommodation services
Notes

1) The provision of food and beverages, although involving the rendering of services and the delivery of
goods, thereby constituting a mixed operation, is classified in this chapter.
2) "Food" (Alimentação) encompasses the supply of food and meals, with or without the offer of beverages. The term
"meal" refers to the portion of food generally consumed at regular times, such as breakfast,
lunch and dinner.
3) The supply of beverages under heading 1.0302 is limited to the offer of beverages.
4) "Accommodation" (Hospedagem) consists of receiving persons who are away from their residences
for the purpose of work, study or leisure, by offering lodging with the services
necessary for the individual's well-being, such as sanitary facilities. It may be understood as what
is conventionally called hotel services (hotelaria), as well as the offer of other types of lodging, for
set periods of time.
5) The services under subheadings 1.0301.1 and 1.0301.2 occur even when provided on trains (dining
cars) or on board ships, and are not to be confused with services provided under contract with a
transport company (catering or provisioning) under subheading 1.0301.32.

                             Chapter 4 - Passenger transport services
Notes

1) Within the scope of this Chapter, the following meanings apply:
  a) Local: the region comprising the urban and rural areas of a municipality and, also, the
  metropolitan region.
  b) Metropolitan area or metropolitan region: the population center resulting from the grouping of two or
  more municipalities integrated economically as well as politically and culturally.
  c) Bus: the collective-transport motor vehicle with seating capacity for more than twenty passengers,
  even if, due to adaptations aimed at greater comfort for such passengers, it may
  carry a smaller number of passengers.
  d) Integrated transport service: one that makes use of more than one vehicle, in one or more
  transport modes.
  e) Waterborne passenger transport services: the mode of transport carried out by
  maritime, lake or river routes.
  f) Sightseeing tours: short-distance tours in urban or rural areas, including
  in the metropolitan area, passing through places of interest for their cultural value, historical
(Fl. 13 of Annex I of Joint Ordinance RFB/SCS No.   , of December, 2018.)


  importance, natural or artificial beauty, unique, rare or mysterious characteristics, and/or also passing
  through places for recreation or entertainment.
  g) Inland navigation: waterborne transport carried out in areas sheltered or partially sheltered from
  bad weather, where storms occurring on the open sea do not affect or barely affect it,
  such as bays, coves, lagoons, rivers, lakes, canals, etc., on domestic or international routes.
  h) Coastal navigation: waterborne transport carried out in open-sea regions where it is still possible
  to sight the coast, limited to a maximum of 20 (twenty) nautical miles from the coast and, in principle, does not
  require the use of orientation instruments, since navigation is carried out by observing land.
  i) Transoceanic navigation: waterborne transport carried out between domestic and foreign ports,
  beyond the limits of visibility of the coast and without other established limits. It requires the use of
  analog or digital location and orientation instruments.
  j) Chartering (Fretamento): the transport service provided by an owner (charterer/lessor) of a vehicle to a third
  party (charterer/lessee), to carry out one or more pre-established trips, with the lessor retaining control
  over the crew and the technical operation of the vehicle involved. The trip-based contract is distinct from the
  time-based contract (heading 1.0405) and from the leasing of a vehicle with operator (heading 1.0404).
  k) Continuous chartering (Fretamento contínuo): the transport service provided by a vehicle owner to legal
  entities for the transport of their employees, to educational institutions or student associations for
  the transport of their students, teachers or members, or to government entities and other
  institutions for the transport of their public servants and staff, with frequency and schedules
  established under contract, for a fixed term, with the lessor retaining control over the
  operator and the technical operation of the vehicle.
  l) Occasional or tourist chartering (Fretamento eventual ou turístico): the service provided by a vehicle owner
  to a third party, which may be a person or group of persons, of an occasional nature and for
  tourist purposes, on a closed circuit, per trip, with the lessor retaining control over the crew/operator and the
  technical operation of the vehicle.
  m) Leasing of passenger transport vehicles with operator: the operation in which the vehicle
  intended for passenger transport is placed at the lessee's disposal for a determined
  period, with the lessor being remunerated according to that time interval. The lessee holds
  control of the vehicle and of the operator's activities.
  n) Time charter party (TCP): a sui generis contract, which does not fall under
  either transport service or leasing, and is characterized by the placing of the vessel and its
  services under the nautical management of the charterer/lessor, that is, manned, equipped and in seaworthy
  condition, at the disposal of the charterer/lessee, so that the latter assumes the commercial management of the naval
  space for a determined period, in exchange for financial remuneration.
2) Passenger transport services include the transport of luggage and animals, among other
items, when transported together with their owners.
3) The following operations are excluded from this Chapter:
  a) "Time chartering" of vessels designed for the transport of cargo, which are classified
  under heading 1.0506 (Chapter 5).
  b) "Bareboat chartering," in which the charterer acquires the possession, use and control of the
  vessel, for a determined period, including the right to designate the master and
  crew, which are classified under subheading 1.1101.15 (Chapter 11).
(Fl. 14 of Annex I of Joint Ordinance RFB/SCS No.       , of December, 2018.)

                                Chapter 5 - Freight transport services
Notes


1) Within the scope of this Chapter, the following meanings apply:
  a) Bulk cargo: unpackaged cargo, almost always homogeneous, which is loaded directly onto
  suitable means of transport, without packaging and in large quantities. This type of cargo has
  three kinds: i) solid bulk, consisting of fragmented solid or plant grain, such as coal, salt,
  wheat grain and iron ore; ii) liquid or liquefied bulk, which includes petroleum and its derivatives,
  such as gasoline and diesel oil, in addition to fuel alcohol, soybean oil and molasses, and liquefied
  bulk, except "liquefied petroleum gas" (LPG).
  b) Live cargo: live animals, vertebrate and invertebrate, planted vegetables, as well as
  microorganisms.
  c) Special cargo: cargo which, due to its shape, safety requirements or compliance with
  parameters, requires special measures to be transported, such as oversized cargo –
  examples of which are large parts for industrial units or power plants – and
  dangerous cargo – understood as any substance or article found in nature or produced by
  any process which, due to its physical-chemical characteristics, poses a risk to human
  health, public safety or the environment.
  d) Non-unitized general cargo: cargo transported loose, in which the goods are presented separately
  and are loaded individually, such as, for example, in bundles, bales, packages, sacks, boxes or
  drums, bearing identification marks and in counted units.
  e) Unitized general cargo: cargo grouped into larger volumes, as occurs, for example, in
  palletized cargo, cargo placed in big boxes or placed in containers.
  f) Container: a structure, generally in the shape of a box fitted with doors, built in carbon
  steel, although it may also be made of aluminum, plastic or fiberglass, among other materials,
  created for the purpose of facilitating the transport of small-sized goods.
  g) Dangerous product: any substance or article found in nature or produced by any
  process which, due to its physical-chemical characteristics, poses a risk to human health, public
  safety or the environment.
(Fl. 18 of Annex I of Joint Ordinance RFB/SCS No.     , of December, 2018.)


  h) Liquefied petroleum gas (LPG): the following liquefied gases: propane, butane, ethylene, propylene,
  butylene, butadiene and other gases covered by subheading 2711.1 of the Mercosur Common
  Nomenclature (NCM), except natural gas (NCM 2711.11.00), and/or mixtures thereof.
  i) Inland navigation: waterborne transport carried out in areas sheltered or partially sheltered from
  bad weather, where storms occurring on the open sea do not affect or barely affect it,
  such as bays, coves, lagoons, rivers, lakes, canals, etc., on domestic or international routes.
  j) Coastal navigation: waterborne transport carried out in open-sea regions where it is still possible
  to sight the coast, limited to a maximum of 20 (twenty) nautical miles from the coast and, in principle, does not
  require the use of orientation instruments, since navigation is carried out by observing land.
  k) Transoceanic navigation: waterborne transport carried out between domestic and foreign
  ports, beyond the limits of visibility of the coast and without other established limits. It requires the use of
  analog or digital location and orientation instruments.
  l) Multimodal freight transport: that which, governed by a single contract, uses two or more
  transport modes, from origin to destination, and is carried out under the responsibility of a
  single multimodal transport operator.
  m) Leasing of freight transport vehicles with operator: the operation in which the vehicle intended
  for freight transport is placed at the lessee's disposal for a determined period, being
  remunerated according to that period. The lessee holds control of the vehicle and of the operator's
  activities.
  n) Time charter party (TCP): a sui generis operation, which does not fall under
  either transport service or leasing, and is characterized by the placing of the vessel and its services
  under the nautical management of the charterer/lessor, that is, manned, equipped and in seaworthy
  condition, at the disposal of the charterer/lessee, so that the latter assumes the commercial management of the naval
  space for a determined period, in exchange for financial remuneration.
2) Waterborne freight transport services (heading 1.0502) are provided by shipping
companies which, with their crews, operate ships and other types of vessels.
3) The following services are excluded from this Chapter:
  a) Cargo handling, which is classified under heading 1.0601.
  b) Warehousing in warehouses or storage units, which is classified under heading 1.0602.
  c) Support navigation, whether port or maritime, which is classified under subheading 1.0605.40.
  d) Transport of water by trucks and other vehicles, which is classified under subheading
  1.0802.30.
  e) Armored car services, which are classified among security services, under subheading 1.1802.40.
4) The following operations are also excluded from this Chapter:
  a) "Time chartering" of vessels designed for the transport of passengers, which are
  classified under heading 1.0405 (Chapter 4).
  b) Leasing of ships and other vessels, without crew ("bareboat chartering"), in which the charterer
  acquires the possession, use and control of the vessel, for a determined period, including the right to
  designate the master and crew, which are classified under subheading 1.1101.15 (Chapter 11).

                               Chapter 6 - Transport support services
Notes

1) Warehousing in warehouses is carried out in general warehouses, which are establishments whose

  and other persons on board) required due to local peculiarities that make the free and safe
  movement of the vessel difficult.

salvage-recovery, and cargo and vessel recovery services.
5) Support navigation services, comprising maritime and port support, are classified under
subheading 1.0605.40.
6) Ancillary services to removal transport, such as
packing and loading of household furniture and office items, which are classified under
Chapter 5 according to the mode of transport, are excluded from this Chapter.

                          Chapter 9 - Financial services and related services
Notes

1) "Investment bank" is a private financial institution, specialized in
temporary equity participation operations, in financing productive activity through the
supply of fixed and working capital and in managing third-party assets.
2) "Supplementary pension services" (previdência complementar) are those offered by supplementary
pension entities, whose main purpose is the administration and execution of pension
benefit plans. These plans are voluntary, of a supplementary nature and organized
autonomously in relation to the general social security regime, and are based on the constitution of reserves
that guarantee the benefit.
 3) "Supplementary pension entities" are classified as closed supplementary pension
entities (EFPCs) and open supplementary pension entities (EAPCs):
  a) EFPCs, commonly called pension funds, are non-profit entities organized
  as foundations or civil associations. They are established exclusively for
  employees of a company or group of companies, for public servants of the Union, the states, the
  Federal District and the municipalities, as well as for members of legal entities of a
  professional, class or sectoral nature, called sponsors.
  b) EAPCs are for-profit entities, established solely as corporations
  (sociedades anônimas), whose purpose is to establish and operate pension benefit plans granted
  in the form of continuous income or a lump-sum payment, accessible to any interested individuals.
4) For the purposes of this Chapter, "financial leasing" corresponds to an operation in which:
  a) The consideration payments and other payments provided for in the contract, owed by the lessee, are
  normally sufficient for the lessor to recover the cost of the leased asset during the
  contractual term of the operation and, additionally, obtain a return on the funds invested.
  b) Maintenance, technical assistance and related services expenses relating to the operability of the leased
  asset are the responsibility of the lessee.
  c) The price for exercising the purchase option is freely agreed, and may even be the
  market value of the leased asset, when the operation is carried out in accordance with Brazilian
  legislation.
5) For the purposes of this Chapter, the following meanings apply:
  a) Reinsurance: the operation of transferring risks from a ceding company (the insurance
  company that enters into a reinsurance operation) to a reinsurer, while the term retrocession designates the
  operation of transferring reinsurance risks from reinsurers to reinsurers, or from
  reinsurers to local insurance companies.
  b) Retrocession: the operation of transferring reinsurance risks from reinsurers, for
  their own protection, to reinsurers or local insurance companies, through
  automatic or facultative contracts, that is, it is the reinsurance of a reinsurance.
  c) Commodity: used in commercial transactions to designate a given good, in raw
  or minimally processed form, produced on a global scale and with homogeneous physical
(Fl. 29 of Annex I of Joint Ordinance RFB/SCS No.      , of December, 2018.)


  characteristics. As a rule, these goods are of agricultural or mineral origin, traded through
  contracts.
  d) Receivables securitization: the operation in which a financial institution holding
  credits ("receivables") assigns those credits to a non-financial institution, established as
  a corporation (sociedade por ações), whose corporate purpose is, exclusively, the acquisition of receivables
  (securitization company).
  e) Factoring (Fomento comercial): the cumulative and continuous provision of credit,
  market and credit-management advisory services, risk selection, management of accounts payable and receivable,
  and the purchase of credit rights arising from term commercial sales or from the provision
  of services.
  f) Trust: legal relationships created, during life or after death, by a given person (the
  settlor or grantor), for the purpose of placing their assets under the control of another person (the
  trustee), who will administer them for the benefit of the beneficiary or for a specific
  purpose.
6) Heading 1.0910 classifies, exclusively, private health care plan services,
which do not provide for the option of free choice of service provider with reimbursement, unlike
health insurance services, which provide for that option and are classified under subheading 1.0903.21.

                           SECTION IV - BUSINESS AND PRODUCTION SERVICES


                           Chapter 12 - Research and development services

Notes

1) In this Chapter, the following definitions apply:
  a) Research: the process aimed at generating, corroborating, or refuting knowledge, which may take
  the form of basic research or applied research.
  b) Basic Research: research in which experimental or theoretical work is developed with the
  aim of obtaining new knowledge about observable phenomena and facts not yet elucidated.
  c) Applied research: research in which original investigative work aims at obtaining new
  knowledge oriented toward specific applications.
  d) Development: the systematic use of scientific or technological knowledge, with the aim of
  obtaining new products or processes or improving and/or perfecting existing ones.
  e) Research and development: the set of creative works, carried out systematically with
  the aim of expanding the base of scientific and technological knowledge, and the use of such knowledge
  to develop new applications, such as new or technologically
  improved products or processes.
2) The term "humanities" includes, for example, languages, literature, history, philosophy, arts, religion, and
theology.

    Chapter 19 - Support services for agriculture, livestock, forestry, fishing, aquaculture, mineral
                    extraction and for the transmission and distribution of electricity, gas and water

Notes

1) In this Chapter, the following are understood as:
  a) Agriculture: the economic activity aimed at the orderly exploitation of plant natural resources
  in natural and protected environments, represented by crop cultivation, formed by four
  segments:
  - Production of temporary crops.
  - Horticulture and floriculture.
  - Production of permanent crops.
  - Production of certified seeds and seedlings.
  b) Livestock: the economic activity aimed at the orderly exploitation of animal resources in
  natural and protected environments, represented by the raising and production of animals, including the
  raising of genetically modified animals, comprising the raising of:
  - Cattle and other large animals, such as horses, donkeys, mules and buffaloes.
  - Swine, goats and sheep.
  - Poultry, such as chickens, ducks, geese, turkeys and ostriches.
  - Other animals with economic significance, such as rabbits, bees, snails and pets.
  c) Forestry production (forestry): the economic activity based on the cultivation of forest
  species, production of logs and the exploitation of non-timber forest products, also including
  the production of forest seedlings, wood products resulting from small
  processing, such as firewood, charcoal and wood chips, or without processing, as in
  posts, stakes and poles.
  d) Fishing: the economic activity that covers the use of fishery resources in marine, brackish
  and fresh waters, aimed at the capture of fish, crustaceans, mollusks and other aquatic organisms
  or products, such as aquatic plants, pearls, corals and sponges.
  e) Aquaculture: the production process that involves the cultivation of aquatic organisms, among them
  fish, crustaceans, mollusks, frogs, aquatic plants, caimans and amphibians, with the aid of techniques that
  intensify the productivity of these organisms beyond their natural capacity for development.
2) Fishing refers to the exploitation, by the public, of aquatic organisms under common ownership, which
differs from aquaculture, which corresponds to the cultivation of aquatic organisms that are
owned.

               Chapter 23 - Services related to human health and social assistance

Notes

1) In heading 1.2301, the following applies:
  a) "Human health care services" cover all forms of services related to human health
  provided in hospitals, outpatient clinics, doctors' offices, clinics, psychosocial care centers,
  mobile emergency care units, transport removals and, also, health services provided in the
  home. They also comprise integrative and complementary practices for human health care.

  b) "Hospital services" are those provided by health care establishments that
  have material and personnel structure intended to handle patient admission, ensure
  basic diagnostic and treatment care, with an organized clinical team and permanent
  assistance provided by physicians, that have nursing services and direct therapeutic
  care to the patient, with availability of laboratory and radiology services, surgery and/or
  delivery services, as well as medical records organized for rapid observation and follow-up of cases.
2) In headings 1.2302, 1.2303 and 1.2304, the following applies:
  a) "Social services" correspond to assistance services to individuals or families, which are
  carried out by government agencies or private institutions and are also provided in the
  home. These activities may or may not include lodging, medical services and education services,
  provided that these are not the main services offered.
  b) "Social assistance" is a non-contributory social security policy, which provides social
  minimums, carried out through an integrated set of public and civil society
  initiative actions, to guarantee the meeting of the population's basic needs.
  c) The objectives of social assistance are: (i) social protection, which aims to guarantee life, to
  reduce harm and to prevent the occurrence of risks, especially: protection of the family, maternity,
  childhood, adolescence and old age; support for needy children and adolescents; promotion of
  integration into the labor market; the qualification and rehabilitation of persons with disabilities and
  the promotion of their integration into community life; and the guarantee of a monthly benefit to persons with
  disabilities and to the elderly who prove they do not have the means to provide for their own maintenance or to have
  it provided by their family; (ii) social-assistance surveillance, which aims to analyze territorially the
  protective capacity of families and, within it, the occurrence of vulnerabilities, threats, victimizations
  and harm; (iii) defense of rights, which aims to guarantee full access to rights within the set of
  social-assistance provisions.
  d) "Social assistance services" are those provided directly at basic and public units
  of social assistance, as well as indirectly at non-public entities and organizations of
  social assistance. Continuous services aimed at improving the population's quality of life and whose
  actions are directed at basic social-protection needs are considered social assistance services or
  assistance services.
  e) Social assistance does not cover only the elderly and persons with disabilities. Social protection is broad, including
  people of any age, whether healthy or not, provided they are exposed to some type of
  social vulnerability.
(Fl. 61 of Annex I to Joint Ordinance RFB/SCS No.         , of December 2018.)

  Chapter 24 - Sewage and waste collection, treatment and disposal services and other environmental
                                          protection services.

Notes:

1) In this Chapter, the following applies:
  a) Waste: is the material, substance, object or good discarded resulting from human activities in
  society, for which final disposal is carried out, proposed to be carried out, or which there is an obligation to carry out. Waste
  may originate from health services, be of industrial or domestic origin, and may
  be characterized as hazardous, non-hazardous, recyclable or non-recyclable.
  b) Hazardousness of a waste: is the characteristic presented by a waste that, due to its
  physical, chemical or infectious-contagious properties, may present risks (i) to public health,
  causing mortality, incidence of diseases or increasing their rates, or (ii) to the environment.
  c) Hazardous waste: is that which presents hazardousness, as defined in item b, and which
  has, among others, one of the following characteristics: flammability, corrosivity, reactivity,
  toxicity, pathogenicity, carcinogenicity, teratogenicity and mutagenicity.
  d) Non-hazardous waste: is that which does not fall within the requirements established for
  hazardous waste.
  e) Health service waste: is that resulting from services related to human or animal
  health care, including home care services and field work; product
  analytical laboratories for health products; morgues, funeral homes and services where
  embalming activities are carried out; forensic medicine services; drugstores and pharmacies, including compounding
  pharmacies; educational and research establishments in the health field; zoonosis control
  centers; distributors of pharmaceutical products, importers, distributors and producers of
  materials and controls for in vitro diagnosis; mobile health care units; acupuncture
  services; tattoo services, among other similar ones.
  f) Recyclable waste: is that waste capable of returning to the production cycle, after having undergone alteration
  of its physical, physico-chemical or biological properties, as occurs with cardboard, paper, plastic,
  glass and metal.
2) Treatment for the reduction or elimination of hazardous products by incineration process is
classified in subposition 1.2404.21, while incineration of non-hazardous products is classified in
subposition 1.2404.33.
3) In position 1.2405, the following applies:
  a) Remediation services: the application of one or more techniques in a contaminated or degraded area,
  with the aim of restoring it in such a way as to allow its reuse within safety
  levels appropriate to the preservation of human health and the environment.
(Fl. 63 of Annex I to Joint Ordinance RFB/SCS No.      , of December 2018.)


  b) Contaminated area: a piece of land, site, facility, building or improvement where the
  presence of chemical substance(s) is found in the air, water or soil, resulting from human activities, in
  concentrations such as to restrict the use of that area for current or intended uses.
4) For the purposes of position 1.2406, urban cleaning and similar services comprise:
  a) The collection, transfer and transport of waste originating from the sweeping and cleaning of public
  streets and thoroughfares.
  b) The sorting for reuse or recycling purposes, treatment, including by composting, and
  final disposal of waste originating from the sweeping and cleaning of public streets and thoroughfares.
  c) The sweeping, weeding and pruning of trees on public streets and thoroughfares and other services
  pertaining to urban public cleaning.
