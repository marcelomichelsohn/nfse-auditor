## Against the prediction, point by point

1. **The door.** Predicted: at least one of {creating the project and uploading ~40 files; pasting `CLAUDE.md` into the instructions; composing the profile line; attaching the XML} would stop her before the first report. What happened: none stopped her — the first report came 12 minutes after she opened the guide — but the path was not the one written. (a) She did not upload the files: she gave the project the unpacked **folder** (the app offers it; the README does not mention it). (b) She could not find `CLAUDE.md` in the folder ("não consegui encontrar esse cloud.MD") and skipped step 3; the instructions stayed empty; the app read the entry file from the folder anyway (transcript, "What she did"). (c) The profile line was the slow step (about 5 minutes) and the ambiguous one: she asked what "perfil" is ("seria a operação do cliente?"), whether `2800308` in the example was a real code or an example, kept the example's `17.19` and added the sheet's `702` beside it, and fetched the CNAE from the client's CNPJ card on a portal rather than the sheet. (d) "Uma nota por conversa" read as if more than one file were expected ("parecia que seria mais de um arquivo"). (e) Two things the README does not mention appeared on screen: the app's folder-access request (she accepted it) and the account's "75% do limite semanal" banner. (f) Attaching the XML: no trip. (g) Model: she chose Opus 5 at high effort — the README said "Opus com esforço alto"; the author's runs were Opus 4.8 at low effort, and the README did not say so.
2. **The notes.** Predicted: at least one municipal XML in "Não lidos". Did not happen: the one note she fed was national-standard XML and was read. One note only; the other three were not run.

5. **Time.** `time.md`.

## What this round measures, in one paragraph
The folder was usable by an outsider on a first contact, on the office's account and a national-standard note, without the author touching anything: a report in the folder's format, on the right provisions, with a FAIL that the prediction had named. What stopped short was not the run but the reading: the report is written for a checker (paths, field names, table ids, a quoted clause per row), and the operator read it as a list of things that are not hers to check. The door held because the app offered a route the README did not describe (the folder), and because the entry file was picked up from the folder when she could not find it. Both are luck, not design, and are the first things to fix.
